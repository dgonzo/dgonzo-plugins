# Scoring for build-vs-buy

Every option gets a score from 1 to 5 on each of seven criteria. A 5 is the best case for the team. Do nothing gets scored too.

## Criteria

| Criterion | Pass | Question | A 5 means |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fit | Comparative | How much of the done-when does it meet? | All of it, with no workaround |
| Time to value | Comparative | How long until the first real user gets value? | Under four weeks |
| Three-year cost | Comparative | What do licence, build and staff cost over three years? | The lowest of the options |
| Run cost | Operator | Who runs it at 2am, and how often? | A named owner and under two pages a month |
| Integration cost | Operator | What does it take to connect it to every system it touches? | Under one week of work and an owner for each touch point |
| Exit cost | Operator | What does it cost to leave? | Data out in an open format within one week |
| 18-month outlook | Futurist | What do the next 18 months do to it? | The option gets cheaper or better with no action |

## Evidence grades

| Grade | Source | Score limit |
|---|---|---|
| Verified | A trial on the team's own data, a reference call the team ran, contract text or the team's own numbers | None |
| Claimed | A demo, a sales deck, a website, an analyst ranking or a vendor's answer to a question | 3 |
| Assumed | The team's own estimate with no source | 3 |

A team's enthusiasm is not evidence. It earns no grade and moves no score.

A Claimed score stays Claimed until the check in the unverified list runs. Then it becomes Verified or drops.

## Checks for common vendor claims

| Claim | Check |
|---|---|
| "It integrates with our stack" | Run the integration on a trial with one real data source |
| "Setup takes a day" | Time a setup by someone on the team, without the vendor's engineer |
| "It scales to our volume" | Load the team's own volume in a trial, or get a reference at that volume |
| "You can export everything" | Export a full dataset in the trial and load it somewhere else |
| "Pricing is flat" | Read the order form and the overage terms in the contract |
| "Customers like us use it" | Call one reference the team picked, not one the vendor picked |

## Run cost questions

Ask these for every option, build included.

- Who gets paged at 2am, by name or team?
- How many hours a month does upkeep take, including upgrades?
- How many systems does it touch, and who owns each touch point?
- What breaks for users when it is down?
- What does leaving cost in weeks, money and data?

## Outlook questions

Trace three steps for each option. Step one is the obvious outcome. Steps two and three are what follows from it.

- Could the cloud platform or an existing tool ship this feature inside 18 months?
- Could the vendor get bought, raise prices or drop the product?
- Will the team's need grow, shrink or move?
- Who maintains the build if its author leaves?
