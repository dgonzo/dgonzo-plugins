# exec-brief templates

Three skeletons. Replace each placeholder in angle brackets. Delete a section only where the note says it is optional.

## Slack message

At most 150 words. No headings. Bold only the ask, with Slack's single asterisks.

```
<Name>, *I need you to <approve, pick or note> <one thing> by <date>.*

<What is blocked until then, and who owns it.>

Options, only if this is a decision:
1. <Option A>. Costs <x>. Risk is <y>. My pick.
2. <Option B>. Costs <x>. Risk is <y>.
If nobody picks by <date>, <consequence>.

Why now:
- <Fact 1> (<source>)
- <Fact 2> (<source>)

Detail: <link>
```

The Detail line is optional. Delete it when no long document exists.

## One-page brief

At most 400 words. Save as `exec-brief-<topic>.md` in the repo folder the user is working in.

```
# <The ask as a short sentence>

To: <Name, role>. From: David Gonzalez. By: <date>.

## Bottom line

<The ask in one sentence, with the date.> <What is blocked and who owns it.>

## Options

| Option | Cost | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| <A>, recommended | <x> | <y> |
| <B> | <x> | <y> |

If nobody picks by <date>, <consequence>.

## Evidence

- <Fact 1>. Source: <link or name>.
- <Fact 2>. Source: <link or name>.
- <Fact 3>. Source: <link or name>.

## Detail

<Link to the long document.>
```

The Options section is optional. Delete it when the ask is awareness. The Detail section is optional. Delete it when no long document exists.

## Approval email

David's proven layout for a spend or contract approval. One screen. Use capitalised labels in place of Markdown headings. Email clients drop Markdown.

```
<Name>,

I need your approval for <thing> at <amount>. <Who> is reviewing <what>. <Party> needs <action> by <date>.

WHAT WE ARE BUYING
   - <fact>

WHY
   - <fact with a number and its source>

WHEN
   - <date or lead time>

COST
   - <amount>, and the baseline it beat

ASK

Approve the <amount> spend. Once <condition>, we will <action>.

Thank you,

--dg
```

## Deck

Seven slides for the exec. Everything else is an appendix the presenters turn to when asked. No decision IDs, tool names or internal coinages on the seven.

| Slide | Holds |
|---|---|
| 1 | The exec's own phrase for the destination, verbatim |
| 2 | The commitment in one sentence with its date. The one ask. Why now in two facts |
| 3 | The exec's own conditions as headings, whole. Under each, the measures with today, target and a verdict word |
| 4 | The fact that makes it the exec's problem, drawn from their own documents |
| 5 | One owner per thing, what each decided, what each still owes |
| 6 | One row per person in the room, what they decide, by when |
| 7 | The verdict and the date |

Every number carries its target and a verdict word in colour. Tables, never card grids. A headline is a claim or a number, never a description of the slide.

## Pass 3 check

Read only the title and the first two lines as the exec. Answer these three questions.

| Question | Pass if |
|---|---|
| What do they want from me? | One verb and one object |
| By when? | A date |
| What happens if I do nothing? | One consequence |

If any answer is missing, the predicted reply is a question. Fix the first two lines and check again.
