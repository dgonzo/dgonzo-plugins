# Headline rubric

Score each headline candidate from 1 to 5 on four criteria. The total is out of 20. A candidate over 10 words is not scored. It is dropped.

## Criteria

| Criterion | Question | Scores 1 | Scores 5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rhythm | Does it read aloud in one breath? | Stumbles or needs a second read | Has a beat a listener can repeat |
| Memorability | Can someone repeat it an hour later? | Abstract nouns only | One concrete image or contrast |
| Provocation | Does it make the reader want the next line? | States the obvious | Raises a question the page answers |
| Ownership | Could an alternative say it truthfully? | All three alternatives could | None of the three could |

Score 3 sits between the two columns.

## Tie break

Ownership breaks a tie. A memorable line a competitor can also say is a line for the competitor.

## Feature list and position

The input usually arrives as a feature list. This invented example shows the change.

Feature list, quoted as the input arrives:

> "A data catalog with lineage, column-level search, Slack alerts, dbt integration and role-based access."

Reductive sentence: "An analyst can see which dashboards break before a column change merges."

Territory check:

| Alternative | Could it say the sentence? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Status quo | No | Teams find breakage after the merge |
| Closest competitor | No | Its lineage shows tables, not dashboards |
| The category | No | Catalogs document data without gating changes |

Headline: "Know what breaks before you merge." Six words.

The five features did not vanish. Lineage and dbt integration become the proof under the headline. Search, alerts and access control go further down the page.
